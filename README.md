# Assessing the Resilience and Policy Controllability of UAE FDI (2025–2026)

## Executive Overview

Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) is a critical pillar of the UAE’s economic diversification and long-term growth strategy. While recent years have demonstrated strong investment momentum, short-term FDI inflows remain exposed to global liquidity conditions, regional oil price dynamics, and shifts in investor confidence.

This project presents a **scenario-based economic advisory analysis** of UAE FDI inflows over the **2025–2026 horizon**. Rather than producing a single-point forecast, the analysis assesses **FDI resilience under alternative global conditions**, identifies the **key sources of near-term volatility**, and evaluates **which policy levers can realistically stabilize or uplift outcomes** within a short-term decision window.

The analysis is designed to mirror how Economic Advisory teams support public-sector decision-makers in investment promotion, macroeconomic strategy, and risk management.

---

## Policy Question and Decision Context

Public-sector leaders and investment authorities are not primarily concerned with whether FDI will be marginally higher or lower in a given year. The core policy questions are:

- How exposed are UAE FDI inflows to external shocks over the next 1–2 years?
- Which macroeconomic channels transmit volatility most strongly into investor behavior?
- To what extent can domestic policy credibility and execution mitigate downside risks?
- What range of outcomes should policymakers plan for under different global conditions?

This project reframes FDI forecasting as a **decision-support problem**, focused on **resilience, controllability, and risk-aware planning**, rather than statistical precision alone.

---

## What This Analysis Is — and Is Not

### What This Analysis Is
- A **short-term (2025–2026) FDI resilience assessment**
- A **scenario-based decision-support framework** for economic strategy and investment promotion
- An application of econometric modeling to **quantify short-run transmission channels** affecting FDI
- A policy-oriented interpretation aligned with Economic Advisory practice in the GCC

### What This Analysis Is Not
- A long-run structural growth or development model
- A precise point forecast of future FDI inflows
- A cross-country benchmarking or panel-data exercise
- An academic econometrics paper focused on model optimization

---

## Why This Matters for the UAE (2025–2026)

The 2025–2026 horizon is particularly relevant for UAE policymakers due to:

- Heightened uncertainty around global financing conditions and capital allocation
- Continued sensitivity of regional liquidity to oil price dynamics
- Strong post-COVID investment momentum that must be actively sustained
- Ongoing competition among global investment destinations for mobile capital

Understanding **where FDI momentum is structurally strong versus where it is vulnerable** allows policymakers to:
- Prioritize high-impact, near-term interventions
- Avoid overreacting to temporary external shocks
- Communicate credible investment narratives grounded in realistic scenarios

This analysis is intended to support **practical, risk-aware economic decision-making**, not abstract forecasting.
